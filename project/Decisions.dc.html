Decisions & tradeoffs
Keep the room. Change what the walls tell you.
1. The shell stays Instead's
The real product is already chat-first: a narrow rail, a centered question, one composer. I tried replacing the rail with a top strip and a drawer — it was more novel, and it made the book feel hidden. A pro should feel their book without clicking. So the rail stays at ~22% of the width, and the work goes into what the rail and the empty chat say in the first three seconds.
2. Who needs me — answered twice, at two depths
In the rail: clients who need you float to the top under "Needs you," with a one-line reason and an amber dot. Everyone else is a single quiet line. In the empty chat: the same people appear as three "Needs you today" chips under the composer — one tap scopes chat to them. Calm clients get no signal at all. Amber is the one colour I added, and it means only this.
3. Clients | Workflows is one toggle, not two places
The old "Workflows" button sent you somewhere else. Now it's the other half of the same rail: switching swaps the list only, chat never reloads, and each tab keeps its own scroll and selection. Cross-client and single-client workflows are grouped separately; a single-client workflow also shows as a small progress pill on that client's row, so you see it from either tab.
4. Selecting is scoping
Clicking a client scopes chat — the same chip-above-composer pattern Instead already uses, now with an ×. Each client keeps its own thread in memory. A workflow can start from chat (the workflow button already in the composer) or from the rail. Both, because pros think in both directions.
5. At 2 clients and at 200
At 2, there's no "Needs you" grouping — it only appears at 3+, when scanning would actually cost time. At 200, the rail scrolls but the top of it is always the short list that matters, and the chat chips cap at three. The home screen never gets taller because the book got bigger.
What I gave up
The drawer idea, and the extra chat space it bought. And compound scope — "this workflow, for this client" — which the chip can't express yet. I'd rather name that gap than ship a scope system nobody can read at a glance.
