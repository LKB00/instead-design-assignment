Decisions & tradeoffs
Why the home screen is chat-first, not rail-first
The bet
The brief says chat is the room, not a feature inside it. A permanent 380px client rail next to chat reads as an inbox with a chat panel bolted on — the opposite of that. So the client list isn't a column here; it's two things instead: a thin "pulse strip" across the top (avatar + first-name chips for anyone who needs attention, nothing else) and a "Book" drawer that opens on demand and closes itself the moment you act on something in it. Chat gets the whole canvas by default.
What this gives up
Permanent visibility of the full roster. A Pro can't see all 8 (or 200) clients without opening the drawer. I think that's the right trade: the brief asks "enough info to know who needs attention without digging" — not "the whole book, always." The pulse strip answers the first at zero cost; the drawer is one tap away for the second.
Why this holds at 2 clients and at 200
The strip is bounded by height, not count — chips scroll horizontally instead of the layout growing vertically. At 2 clients with nothing urgent, the strip goes quiet ("Book's quiet right now"). At 200 clients with 40 needing attention, the strip is identical in height — it just scrolls further. The drawer's own "Needs attention" grouping (added earlier) is the second-level answer once you do open the book: group when there are enough to lose track of, don't when there aren't.
Chat ↔ client list ↔ workflows
Selecting a client (chip or drawer row) scopes chat — a pinned pill shows what's active, with its own remembered thread. A workflow can be started the same way from either direction: from the drawer's Workflows tab, or straight from chat via the "+" in the composer — because the brief asks whether a Pro can start one from chat, from a client, or both, and the honest answer is both, so the UI shouldn't force a single path.
What I'd flag as unresolved
Two scope types (client, workflow) share one pill for now — clean, but a Pro doing both at once (a workflow, for a client) has no way to say so yet. I'd rather ship the simpler version and name the gap than build a compound-scope system nobody asked to see.
